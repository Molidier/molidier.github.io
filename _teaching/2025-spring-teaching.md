In Spring 2025, I served as a **Teaching Assistant and Lab Developer** for *Microarchitecture Design and Verification*, one of the first dedicated hardware design courses at Nazarbayev University.

## Course Overview

This undergraduate course introduces students to:

- Microarchitecture design principles  
- RTL implementation in Verilog/SystemVerilog  
- FPGA-based prototyping and verification workflows  

By the end of the semester, each student team was expected to design, implement, and validate a working processor on an FPGA board.

## Responsibilities

As a TA and lab developer, I:

- **Supported Kazakhstan’s first hardware design course**, helping **18 students** complete working processor designs.  
- **Designed two hands-on FPGA labs**:
  - UART peripheral lab on the **Altera DE1-SoC**  
  - Load-Store Unit (LSU) design and memory interface lab  
- **Mentored 20+ students** in weekly Q&A and debugging sessions, guiding:
  - RTL design and simulation  
  - Signal tracing and waveform analysis  
  - Integration of peripherals with the processor core  

## Lab & Verification Infrastructure

To streamline project development, I:

- Built a **cocotb-based verification framework** for Tiny Tapeout-style designs, allowing students to:
  - Run automated testbenches
  - Visualize waveforms quickly with common tools
- Reduced **onboarding time** for the verification environment from about **one week to two days**, enabling students to focus more on design and experimentation rather than tooling.

## Impact

This teaching experience strengthened my interest in:

- Hardware education and open-source tooling  
- Making microarchitecture and verification more approachable for undergraduate students  
- Bridging the gap between **research-level design flows** and **student-friendly infrastructure** in the classroom.
